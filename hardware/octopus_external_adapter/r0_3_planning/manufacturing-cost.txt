Decision supported by the evidence

The current r0.2 quote cannot support a US$15 retail assembled adapter plus cable. The parent-reported PCBWay quote is $336.24 for five bare boards plus $29 assembly: ($336.24 + $29) / 5 = $73.05 each before components, cable, freight, tax, testing, packaging, and selling costs. This research did not independently re-open the user's quote or change its browser state.

For r0.3, target two copper layers, all SMT on ONE face, 1 oz copper, 1.6 mm FR-4, >=0.1524 mm (6 mil) track width AND copper spacing, and >=0.30 mm drill with >=0.60 mm via diameter. Preserve purple, keyed cable output, power selector, and the requested 5/12/24 V indication. Recheck the actual Gerbers and component lands before selecting these quote options. Why the old quote is expensive

1. Fine copper geometry: PCBWay advertises standard minimum trace and spacing as 0.1 mm/4 mil. Its order form offers 3/3, 4/4, 5/5, and 6/6 mil options. The actual r0.2 custom U20 land has 0.100 mm gaps at pin 1 to pin 11 and pin 8 to pin 9, plus 0.120/0.125 mm gaps elsewhere. 4 mil is 0.1016 mm and 6 mil is 0.1524 mm. Therefore even choosing 4/4 mil for the existing 0.100 mm geometry is inaccurate. The replacement power package and footprint must truly meet the new rules. Do not alter manufacturer lands solely to lower the displayed quote.

Practical home-assembly layout gate (engineering recommendation)

Two copper layers and two populated faces are different choices. A two-layer board can have every SMT part on top and an empty bottom solder-mask surface. That permits flat bottom contact with a hotplate before fitting through-hole parts. Reflow all SMT first; inspect and test the power section; then hand-solder StepStick headers, JST output, selector and power terminal. Connector plastics and projecting pins must not be sandwiched against the heating surface.

The current r0.2 double-sided SMT board is not suited to straightforward direct-contact hotplate assembly: once one face is populated, flipping it puts parts against the plate and removes flat contact. It would require a qualified fixture/preheater/hot-air or oven process, with a thermal profile for both sides and component-retention checks. A hotplate alone does not make that process proven. A single-SMT-face redesign avoids this extra tooling and process development.

Preserve the known StepStick mating geometry: 15.24 mm board width, 12.70 mm between header rows, 2.54 mm pin pitch, and physical omission of the VM pin. Width alone does not prove compatibility. Keep the top solder-tail envelopes clear, ensure the JST latch can be operated, and point power-terminal wires outward. Existing r0.2 is 38.10 mm long with 17.78 mm extension beyond the old 20.32 mm body. Any longer single-face redesign must be checked on the exact host board, against adjacent drivers, connectors, fans and enclosure walls. Do not widen over neighboring slots or promise all BTT/SKR hosts.

Use a provisional <=50 mm length packing target to fit the whole bare board on the common 50 x 50 mm plate, but do not freeze dimensions before a real single-face placement/routing proof. A 55 x 55 mm plate gives modest margin. If the part count cannot fit while preserving electrical and mechanical clearances, simplify the circuit or revise the mechanical plan; do not shrink clearances or invent a finished layout. Top/bottom ground copper still needs a short buck input loop, short switching loop, adequate return paths and thermal checks.

Prefer visible-lead packages such as SOIC/TSSOP/SOT-23 and 0603/0805 passives over tiny leadless packages when compatible with size and budget. A stencil helps repeatability and should be included in prototype tooling cost. A magnifier, fine tweezers, flux, soldering iron, and paste with a real manufacturer thermal profile are still needed. Temperature shown by a plate is its sensor temperature, not proof of solder-joint temperature; verify the paste's profile on a sacrificial assembly. These are assembly-process requirements, not a claim that any model has been tested with this board.

Retail target and purchase gate

US$15 is a retail target for one tested assembled adapter plus its matching cable, not a five-unit prototype budget. Recurring delivered PCB + BOM + cable + assembly + test/yield + packing + selling costs + margin must fit within $15. Reusable hotplate and tools are separate initial expenditure; small Amazon multipacks create upfront cash cost and leftover inventory even when per-used-part cost looks small. A target cost calculation must keep both totals.

Before a new bare-board order: complete the revised circuit and true two-layer layout, verify all footprints at >=6/6 mil including pad-to-pad gaps, verify ERC/DRC and the original socket pinout, prove top-side assembly access, price the exact purple/finish/quantity Gerbers plus stencil/freight, and source every actual MPN with sufficient voltage ratings. The requested 5/12/24 V indication must be in the BOM and power budget, and must identify measured voltage or explicitly label a selected setting; LEDs alone do not prove compatibility with every external driver. No substitutions or an Amazon-only BOM should be claimed complete until each electrical part is actually available and matches the qualified package/specification.

Recommended next commercial comparison: same completed design, purple throughout, quantities 5/10/25/100, standard lead time, no special stackup/impedance/via fill, and explicit bare PCB versus assembly/parts/cable costs. Record ENIG and a separately reviewed HASL alternative as distinct quotes. The final decision should use delivered cost, not the banner price or bare-board area.
