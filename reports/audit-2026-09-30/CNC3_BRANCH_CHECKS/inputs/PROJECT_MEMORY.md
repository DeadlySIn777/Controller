Exact components/ratings remain a design task, not a claimed completed protection circuit. - **Latest explicit user requirement (28 September 2026): the controller must run at least eight motors and be expandable to up to twelve.** Eight motor channels (differential STEP/DIR with individual enable and alarm) are now the minimum product capability, not a candidate; channels 9-12 are a required expansion path. How the expansion is provided (fully populated board, plug-in module or a second endpoint) is a design and cost decision still to be made. This supersedes the six-joint/two-encoder study as the product target. Its results remain historical baseline evidence and must not be relabelled as eight- or twelve-channel evidence. Motor channels are not axes (a dual-motor gantry uses two). The current 64-byte CNC1 frame cannot carry twelve channels, so the protocol, gateware, driver, electrical allocation, power budget and cost all need new work.
- **User decisions confirmed 28 September 2026** (an advisor's recommendation that the user re-sent as their answer and then explicitly authorised): eight motors standard; motors 9-12 on a plug-in expansion module, with a twelve-motor version sold with that module factory-fitted; the FPGA on a replaceable motion card with its clock, configuration flash and supervisory circuitry, behind a fixed wiring base whose field terminals never move (pluggable terminal blocks may also be used where they help replace the base itself); individual enable and alarm preserved on every motor channel, with any unsupported drive behaviour listed as an explicit exception, and a global power interlock never counted as per-channel enable; all 24 general inputs and 16 general outputs preserved (serial transport to I/O cards allowed only with identity, sequence/freshness and CRC validation before outputs change); the 20 ms fault-to-inhibit watchdog requirement retained, not relaxed to about 72 ms to keep a drawn part; a US$400 cost target for the defined eight-motor package including the selected touchscreen and a $15 AI setup allowance, with the expansion costed separately and overruns reported rather than interfaces removed; Codex owns schematics, power/protection, connectors, PCB and hardware BOM, and Claude owns FPGA logic, the CNC1 protocol/driver and configuration-service integration, both working from one agreed interface specification; commit a baseline and then work on separate branches/worktrees with controlled merges. Acceptance test: replace every designated service module with power off, without soldering, disturbing individual machine wires, manually flashing firmware or losing machine configuration; recovery works offline, rejects incompatible hardware and never resumes motion automatically.

Power-off replacement, setup/calibration invalidation and deliberate restart are required; saved calibration does not preserve verified position after power loss. See docs/PLASMA_MODULE_SYSTEM.md.

- Latest repairability requirement: replace a failed section without rewiring individual conductors or rebuilding the machine setup. Compute, motion, field I/O, process interfaces and power/service are candidate service boundaries, subject to connector/timing/power/cost qualification. See docs/MODULAR_REPAIR_AND_MEMORY.md.
- Retain a protected removable machine-memory cartridge containing the actual versioned configuration, mirrored to main storage and exportable offline. Keep machine settings separate from each module's own identity/factory calibration and from firmware/Linux images. Measure capacity before selecting EEPROM/flash; FRAM is a workload-dependent option. Two validated committed records must survive an interrupted save; conflicting identities or equally current different records must not be silently selected.
- Supported replacement modules should arrive programmed (or need no firmware), restore compatible assignments and guide only affected verification. First release replacement is power-off service; outputs remain inhibited, live position/homing and job authority are not restored, and stale requests cannot restart machining. Replacement measurement electronics bring their own factory calibration. Existing same-store backups remain distinct from a deliberate new-compute import bound to the correct machine. Offline recovery and affordable available spares are product requirements, not present procurement guarantees.
