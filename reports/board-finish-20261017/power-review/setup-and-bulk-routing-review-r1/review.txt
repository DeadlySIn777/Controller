The bottleneck is broader than individual airwires. The saved stage4 board uses three busy signal-routing layers and hundreds of mixed ground/power zone roles. The board is450 x 235 mm with2,723 footprints. All six native copper layers are nominal35 um and marked signal, but In1/In2/In4 together have only63 track segments and436 source-zone entries. Source planes need explicit domain roles, finite source connections and wide current paths before a global signal router can finish reliably.

The actual project has26 netclasses, all with the same0.15 mm clearance,0.2 mm track and0.6/0.3 mm via defaults. The0.6 mm all-layer interdomain clearance lives in later custom DRU rules, including plated barrels and24 galvanic classes. A raw DSN/default-class route can therefore look complete while missing real isolation, return and current requirements. Native zones are source polygons; they cannot substitute for actual filled/source-rooted copper in return qualification.

Keep isolation, actual current ratings, all 12 motor interfaces, package/drill geometry and scoped native exceptions. Separate optional host guards/planning reserves from native limits; do not globally shrink rules to force completion. First assign and connect broad per-domain In1/In4 reference regions and current-sized power corridors, then allow coordinated bank-level placement and rip-up of crossing signal/control paths on an isolated revision. Preserve the electrical/fault contract and validate the new paths, rather than making every historical copper path immutable during a global redesign. Do not merge galvanic domains.

The native stackup is not yet quote-aligned: six 35 um copper layers plus0.22/0.33/0.22/0.33/0.22 mm dielectric sum 1.53 mm, or 1.55 mm including the two masks, while general thickness says 1.6 mm. Reconcile this with PCBWay stackup and minimum copper/plating before impedance, current and thermal signoff. The nominal35 um setting does not certify earlier conservative 25 um calculations.
