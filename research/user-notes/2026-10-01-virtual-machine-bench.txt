The big move is to connect the first four layers so the real LinuxCNC thinks it is controlling a real machine.

### 1. LinuxCNC → actual CNC3 driver → simulated FPGA

You already have the right foundation for this.

Use Verilator to compile the actual FPGA RTL into an executable C++ model. Verilator specifically supports converting Verilog/SystemVerilog into executable C++/SystemC models with tracing and coverage. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

Then the CNC3 transport talks to that instead of `/dev/spidev`.

So LinuxCNC doesn't know it's simulated:

```text
LinuxCNC
   ↓
real CNC3 HAL driver
   ↓
simulated SPI transport
   ↓
ACTUAL FPGA RTL in Verilator
```

Not a fake FPGA written in Python.

### 2. Put a virtual machine behind the FPGA

This is the cool part.

Every STEP/DIR output feeds a simulated motor/axis:

```text
STEP0 → X ballscrew → X position
STEP1 → Y-left
STEP2 → Y-right
STEP3 → Z
...
STEP11 → joint 11
```

The model generates back:

- ABZ encoder signals
- home switches
- limit switches
- drive alarms
- probe triggers
- spindle encoder
- frequency inputs

Then we can simulate actual machine mechanics.

Give each joint configurable:

`steps/rev × microsteps × screw lead × gear ratio × travel × acceleration × backlash`

And inject problems:

- missed steps
- encoder count disagreement
- reversed motor
- reversed encoder
- gantry side lag
- limit switch stuck
- home switch never triggers
- drive alarm
- excessive following error
- backlash
- motor unable to achieve commanded acceleration

That means your setup wizard and AI can be tested against broken machines before you even have the PCB.

### 3. Hammer all 12 channels simultaneously

This becomes an automated acceptance suite.

I would require simulations like:

- 12 channels at low speed
- 12 at 100 kHz
- 12 at 500 kHz
- 12 at 1 MHz
- 12 at the proposed 2 MHz hardware target
- constantly changing velocities
- all channels reversing
- multiple gantry pairs
- encoder activity simultaneously
- probe hit during maximum motion
- full I/O traffic at the same time

And count every edge.

If the simulation requests 50,000,000 pulses over a long test, the generated count must be exactly 50,000,000 unless a deliberately injected fault occurred.

Then assert:

```text
→ output permission removed
→ motion stops

```

Run thousands of randomized combinations overnight.

### 5. SPICE the electrical blocks

Do not try to SPICE all 880 footprints as one monster simulation. That would be miserable.

Break the motherboard into electrical test benches:

- 24 V power entry
- 5 V/3.3 V/1.2 V converters
- FPGA reset
- both hardware watchdogs
- permission latches
- STEP/DIR drivers
- encoder receivers
- 24 V input stages
- TPS274C65 outputs
- analog inputs
- 0–10 V outputs
- probe circuitry
- CAN/RS-485 protection

KiCad has ngspice-based circuit simulation, and TI provides PSpice-for-TI with device models, Monte Carlo/worst-case analysis and mixed-signal/power simulation. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

For every important analog/power block run:

`min voltage / nominal / max voltage`

plus component tolerances and temperature corners.

### 6. FPGA place-and-route timing is mandatory

RTL simulation can say the logic is correct while the real FPGA can't meet timing.

So after RTL:

> Efinity synthesis → placement → routing → static timing.

Efinix's flow specifically performs place-and-route/timing analysis, and its timing-closure tooling addresses congestion and timing optimization. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}

For your target I would require timing closure comfortably beyond the actual 50 MHz FPGA core requirement.

### 7. Simulate the whole user experience too

This matters for your product.

LinuxCNC's HAL tools support assembling and exercising configurations without actual machine hardware, which is useful for this layer. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="5"}

- ridiculous 12-joint test machine

If an update breaks one of them, CI fails.
